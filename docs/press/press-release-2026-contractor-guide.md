## Houston Superior Painting Releases 2026 Houston Home Painting Preparation and Contractor Selection Guide

*Free online guide explains what surface preparation should appear in a written painting estimate and introduces the company's 10-Point Preparation Standard*

**CYPRESS, Texas** — Houston Superior Painting, a Cypress-based painting company founded in 2019, today released its *2026 Houston Home Painting Preparation and Contractor Selection Guide*, a free resource for Greater Houston homeowners who are planning an interior or exterior painting project and comparing contractors.

The guide is available at https://houstonsuperiorpainting.com/houston-painting-contractor-guide.

The guide walks homeowners through the questions to ask before hiring a painter, how to read a written estimate, and which preparation steps should be listed on it. It also explains that Texas does not issue a state license for residential painting contractors, and what homeowners can check instead: proof of general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage, a written warranty, and an itemized scope of work.

### Why documented preparation matters in Houston

Houston's heat, humidity and frequent rain are hard on painted surfaces. Moisture, mildew and peeling are common problems on local homes, and early paint failure usually traces back to preparation rather than to the paint itself: coatings applied over dirty, damp, chalky or loose surfaces do not bond well.

Because preparation happens before the finish coats go on, it is also the part of a painting job a homeowner has the hardest time checking afterward. The guide recommends that homeowners ask every contractor to put preparation in writing, step by step, so bids can be compared on the same scope.

### The Houston Superior Painting 10-Point Preparation Standard

The guide introduces the Houston Superior Painting 10-Point Preparation Standard, the company's internal checklist for preparing a home before painting. It is the company's own process, not an industry certification or a third-party standard. Not every step applies to every surface; the written estimate states which steps a given project includes. 1. **Surface-condition inspection.** Moisture, peeling, rot, cracks, stains and the existing coating type are checked before the scope is written.
2. **Protection of surrounding areas.** Floors, furniture, fixtures, landscaping and roofs are covered and masked.
3. **Cleaning.** Dirt, grease, chalk and mildew are removed so coatings can bond.
4. **Removal of loose or failing coatings.** Failing paint is scraped and sanded back to a sound edge.
5. **Drywall, wood and masonry repairs.** Damage is patched, replaced or repaired before coating.
6. **Sanding and surface profiling.** Glossy surfaces are dulled and patches feathered smooth.
7. **Caulking and gap treatment.** Failed caulk is removed and joints and gaps are sealed.
8. **Correct primer selection.** Primer is matched to the surface and the existing coating.
9. **Coating application according to the written scope.** The products, sheens and coat counts in the estimate are what goes on the wall.
10. **Final inspection, touch-ups and cleanup.** The work is walked with the homeowner before the final payment.
